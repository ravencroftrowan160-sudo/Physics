# Conceptual Exercise 2.1

## State the Problem
A car at rest at a traffic light starts moving faster and faster when the
light turns green. The car reaches the speed limit in 4 s, continues at
the speed limit for 3 s, then, approaching a second, red, traffic light,
slows down and stops in 2 s. At the light, the car waits for 1 s until
it turns green. Meanwhile, a cyclist approaching the first green light
keeps moving without slowing down or speeding up. She reaches
the second stoplight just as it turns green. Draw a motion diagram
for the car and another for the bicycle as seen by an observer on the
ground. If you place one diagram below the other, it will be easier to
compare them.

## Expectation
Sketch and Translate: Visualizethemotion for the car andforthebicycleas seenby the observer
on the ground. The car and the bicycle are our objects of interest.
The motion of the car has four distinct parts:
1. starting at rest and moving faster and faster for 4 s;
2. moving at a constant rate for 3 s;
3. slowing down to a stop for 2 s; and
4. sitting at rest for 1 s.
The bicycle moves at a constant rate with respect to the ground for the
entire time.

## Timeline Breakdown (Total: 10 Seconds)

| Time Frame | Car Motion State | Bicycle Motion State |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0s – 4s** | **Constant Acceleration** (Velocity vectors increasing) | **Constant Velocity** (Steady pacing) |
| **4s – 7s** | **Constant Velocity** (Maintaining speed limit) | **Constant Velocity** (Steady pacing) |
| **7s – 9s** | **Constant Deceleration** (Velocity vectors decreasing) | **Constant Velocity** (Steady pacing) |
| **9s – 10s** | **At Rest / Stationary** (Velocity = 0) | **Constant Velocity** (Steady pacing) |

## Conceptual Analysis
1. **The Spacing Paradox:** The car's dots grow further apart as it speeds up, maintain wide spacing at its top speed, and crowd closely together as it brakes. The bicycle's dots maintain identical geometric spacing from start to finish.
2. **The Catch-Up Mechanism:** Although the car achieves a much higher peak velocity than the bicycle between seconds 4 and 7, its 2-second deceleration phase and 1-second idle time allow the steady, unchanging pace of the cyclist to perfectly close the distance gap exactly at $t = 10\text{ s}$.
